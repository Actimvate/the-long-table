How it works

Four steps, one month, no invoices.

  1. 01

    You submit

    Send your book through the form below. Print, ebook, or audio, any genre, any publisher or none. A human reads the first pages of everything that comes in. Nothing is filtered by an algorithm or a budget.

  2. 02

    The committee selects

    Five readers, chaired by Dr Timothy Jo, argue it out and pick the coming month's two books on the 26th, and set its sittings the same day. Their job ends at choosing: the committee never reads on the house's behalf. If yours is chosen, you hear from us within 48 hours.

  3. 03

    The whole house reads

    No groups, no tracks, no splitting the list between us. When a book is picked, all of us read it: the same book, the same month, all 305 members at their own pace. While a book is on, the long table opens two days a week for quiet reading and small-table talk.

  4. 04

    The sitting, then the ratings

    Twice a month we close a book with a full-house sitting: 60 to 90 readers in the room, a video link open for everyone else. Afterwards, members leave honest ratings and reviews where your book is sold. A typical cycle lands 45 to 70 ratings within six weeks.

One honesty note: readers DNF books they’re not enjoying, and about one in five picks doesn’t get finished by everyone. You’ll get that feedback privately and kindly, because it’s worth more than a performed five stars.

The committee

Five readers pick the books. This is the chair.

Chair · selection committee

Dr Timothy Jo

chair since January 2026 · reading with the house since 2023

Timothy Jo finished a doctorate in twentieth-century fiction at twenty-six and now lectures two days a week, which leaves five for the thing he actually spends his time on: reading. He joined the house in 2023 through the front door, one email, one book he loved, and took one of the two committee seats the house always reserves for members in their first year.

He was elected chair this January, at twenty-eight, after two years on the committee. He runs the monthly argument, keeps the longlist to five, and casts the deciding vote when the room splits. Selection is all the committee does: once a book is chosen, the whole house reads it, Timothy included. His rule is famous among the 305:

“If the book doesn't ask a question, it doesn't get a sitting.”

The honesty clause

We don’t do paid praise. There is no version of this where money buys a kinder rating. If readers don’t finish your book, you’ll hear it privately, kindly, and with specifics. And donations are never tied to selection: choosing books is the committee’s only job, and money never enters that room.

Eligibility: released in the last 12 months, or quietly overlooked for longer. Any genre, any format. Self-published and small-press books make up over half our archive.

The perk authors remember · Author evenings

When a book moves the house, we invite its author.

Some books earn more than a rating. For those, we host an author evening: the questions come from members, collected across the two reading weeks and trimmed down to the ones the whole room wants asked. Most evenings are live at the long table; when distance wins, the author answers in writing and their answers run in the reader report. No softballs, no script. Members sign the questions they want put.

Join as a reader

Joining is a conversation, not a form.

Write to thelongtablebookclub@gmail.com with the last book you truly loved. That’s the whole application. We’re not screening for degrees, just for people who finish things. A human replies within a week, usually faster.

You get two books a month worth your time, a table of people who argue well, and snacks. Membership is free; contributions to the refreshments fund are welcome and never tracked against your name.

The deal, plainly

  • Read at least one pick every three months. Most of us read both, because the house talks about them all month

  • Come when you can: the long table opens two days a week, and each book closes with one full-house sitting

  • Leave an honest rating for every book you finish. That's the whole engine

Roughly 200 of our 305 readers joined the same way: one email, one book, one slightly overlong sentence about why they loved it. Write yours.

Questions

Asked often, answered honestly.

Something we missed? Write to thelongtablebookclub@gmail.com, a person answers, usually within two days.

What does it cost an author?

Nothing. No fee, no commission, no obligation of any kind. The community is funded by voluntary reader contributions and the occasional author donation, and the two are never connected: donating never changes who gets picked, and not donating never hurts a book's chances.

How are the two books selected, and who reads them?

A committee of five, chaired by Dr Timothy Jo, reviews every submission plus member suggestions and argues until two books survive the month. But the committee's job ends at choosing. There are no groups here: once a book is picked, the whole house reads it, all 305 of us, both books, every month. Nobody gets to take one book while someone else takes the other.

What if readers don't finish my book?

Then you'll hear that privately, kindly, and with specifics: which chapters lost people, and where. We don't do paid praise and we don't perform enthusiasm. An honest 4.1 with thirty real reviews outsells a hollow 5.0 over time, and we'd rather build you the first one.

Do you take self-published or small-press books?

Over half our archive is self-published or small press. Print, ebook, and audiobook all work. Ebooks are the easiest for members; print makes the nicest photos.

When and where does the house meet?

The long table opens two days a week: quiet reading, small-table talk, and the arguments that spill out of both. Twice a month, each pick closes with one full-house sitting, in the room with a video link open for members who can't travel. Sittings are where ratings get decided, so we guard them carefully.

How is the community funded?

Reader contributions and occasional voluntary author donations cover refreshments and room costs only, about $290 a month across two open days and two sittings. The ledger is published every month in the section below. Nobody is paid; not the committee, not the chair, not the founder.
