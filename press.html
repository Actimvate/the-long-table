The Long Table Turns Four: 88 Books, 305 Readers, and Not a Penny Charged to Authors

The London readers' collective, founded in the autumn of 2022, marks four years on the same two rules: everyone finishes the whole book, and no author ever pays for the shelf space.

The Long Table, a readers' collective of 305 members, is celebrating its fourth anniversary this September. Founded around a single long table in the autumn of 2022, the club has since read and rated 88 books, two at a time, month after month, with a rest every December. In a typical cycle 117 of its members are actively reading, and every one of those books closed with what the club calls a full-house sitting: an evening where the whole room, having read the book cover to cover, argues about it in person.

The anniversary coincides with the club's monthly selection night. On the 26th of every month, a five-reader committee chaired by Dr Timothy Jo picks the coming month's two books from public submissions and sets the sitting dates the same evening. On 26 September the committee will choose the October books, and the club's fourth year quietly becomes its fifth.

“Four years in, the numbers I am proudest of are the boring ones. Eighty-eight books finished, not skimmed. Three hundred and five names on the roster. And nothing taken from authors, because the moment a writer pays for a review, the review stops being honest.”

Dr Timothy Jo, chair of the selection committee

The club charges authors nothing at any stage: no submission fee, no listing fee, no pay-to-play tier. It is funded entirely by voluntary pledges from its own readers toward refreshments, with the accounts published to a public ledger each month; the ledger currently carries $38.20 forward into October. When a book is selected, the reward for its author is a house of readers who finish it and leave honest ratings on the retail and review platforms where it is sold, alongside an author evening with the room.

The fifth year holds the club's 100th book, expected in early 2027, a growing programme of author evenings, and the December rest the members have taken every year since the founding. Submissions from authors remain open year-round and free, and new readers can join at any point in the cycle.

On the 26th of Every Month, Five Readers Pick Two Books: An Open Call to Authors Who Are Tired of Paying

Submissions to The Long Table are free year-round. The committee reads everything, replies to everyone, and the chosen books get a full house of 117 readers who finish them.

Every month on the 26th, the five readers of The Long Table's selection committee, chaired by Dr Timothy Jo, sit down and argue. By the end of the night they have picked the coming month's two books from the public submissions pile, and they have set the sitting dates for both. Their job ends at choosing: the committee never reads on the house's behalf, and if your book is the one chosen, you hear from the club within 48 hours.

The submission itself takes two minutes on the club's website and asks for the basics: title, genre, format, a link, and anything the author wants the committee to know. There is no fee at any point and no list a writer can buy onto. Every submission is read by the committee in the first week of the month, and every author receives a reply by the 10th, a real reply rather than a confirmation bot. Print, ebook and audiobook are all eligible, and self-published titles sit in the same pile as everything else.

What a selected author receives is unusual in the current landscape. One hundred and seventeen active readers commit to finishing the book, not sampling it. The book closes with a full-house sitting where the author is invited to be present for an evening of questions. Honest ratings appear on the platforms where the book is sold. And the thing the author is never asked for is money.

“We are not a review service, we are a room. Authors cannot pay us, which is exactly why the prize is worth having: a hundred-plus readers who will actually finish your book and say what they honestly thought, where it is sold.”

Dr Timothy Jo, chair of the selection committee

The next selection night falls on Saturday 26 September 2026, when the October books will be chosen and their sitting dates fixed. Submissions are open now and remain open every month of the year except the December rest.

The Book Club Where Books Actually Get Finished: 88 Titles in Four Years, No Half-Read Endings

Most reading groups quietly stall by chapter three. This one only sits once every page is turned, and its members say that is the whole point.

Reading groups have a familiar failure mode: the first meeting is enthusiastic, the second is half-attended, and by the third the book is abandoned in favour of wine. The Long Table, a 305-member readers' collective in London, built its entire rulebook around preventing exactly that. The result is a club with a completion habit: 88 books read and rated since the autumn of 2022, none of them abandoned at the halfway mark.

The mechanism is strict but simple. The whole house reads the same two books each month, cover to cover. The table opens two days a week, Thursdays and Saturdays, for readers working through the month's picks. A book only closes with a full-house sitting, an evening where everyone in the room has finished the same pages and the argument is live. The selection committee, five readers chaired by Dr Timothy Jo, chooses the books but never reads on the house's behalf, so the room always reads for itself.

The numbers behind the habit are published, not claimed. Two books a month since the founding, with a December rest, have produced 88 completed titles across literary fiction, memoir, horror, poetry, and self-published debuts. One hundred and seventeen readers are active in a typical cycle. Ratings are left where the books are sold, including when they are mixed, which the club treats as the price of being taken seriously.

The club's running costs are equally transparent. It charges authors nothing and charges readers no membership fee; refreshments are covered by voluntary pledges from members, and the ledger is published monthly on the site, currently carrying $38.20 into October 2026.

“Finishing is the culture; everything else follows from it. A room where everyone finished the book argues better, rates honestly, and comes back next month. That is the entire secret, and it is not really a secret at all.”

Dr Timothy Jo, chair of the selection committee

Readers can join at any point in the cycle through the club's website, and authors can submit a book for consideration free of charge. The next selection night is on 26 September 2026, when the October books will be announced.
